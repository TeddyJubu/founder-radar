The problem

Version 1 kept finding companies that were already famous

The client's words, twice: "a lot of them were founded 5–7 years ago, and quite a few have already raised funding… there's a good chance these funds have already come across them, and I'm not really adding much value."

The cause was structural, not a bug. Version 1 read VC portfolio pages and news round-ups. A portfolio page is a record of companies that already raised — by definition, a lagging indicator. Scouting from one is guaranteed to be late.

The fix in one sentence. Companies House lets you search by incorporation date, for free. A company incorporated ninety days ago cannot be six years old. That is a guarantee the register enforces, not a filter we hope works.

Where companies come from

Two ways in, deliberately different

Track A — Signal first

Someone already vetted these
  • University spinout announcements
  • Accelerator cohorts & demo days
  • Innovate UK & UKRI grant awards
  • Curated UK tech news

Young and credible — but other scouts can see them too. Good volume, moderate edge.

Track B — Register first

Nobody has vetted these
  • Newly incorporated UK tech companies
  • Qualified by share-issue filings (a pre-seed round on the public record)
  • Founders with prior directorships
  • Cross-matched to grants and websites

Lower yield, very high edge. This is where the client finds things no fund has seen.

The highest-value query in the whole system. "Incorporated in the last 90 days" + "tech sector code" + "in one of our regions" + SH01 filed since incorporation. An SH01 is a share allotment — on a young company, that is a pre-seed round being papered. It hits the public register within days, it's free, and no portfolio-page scraper will ever see it.

The trap this design had to solve. A company register entry contains none of the things we score on — no sector, no stage, no founder type, no traction. Left alone, every register-sourced company would score on location alone, fail the "do we know enough?" check, and sit in a watchlist nobody reads. So the system derives those facts from free public evidence: industry code → sector, postcode → region, share-allotment filing → stage, prior directorships → founder type, grant award → traction. That derivation step is what turns registry-first discovery from an idea into a working pipeline. And a new company with none of that evidence isn't rejected — it just waits, and gets re-checked every morning until it earns a place.

Scoring

Two numbers, not one

Version 1 ranked on fit alone. That is exactly what produced a list of perfect-fit companies every fund already knew. A scout's value is information the fund doesn't have — so that has to be a number too.

Fund Fit
88

How well it matches this fund's stated criteria — sector, stage, geography, founder type, traction. Weighted by the client's own matrix, which lives in the sheet where he can edit it.

Discovery Edge
90

How likely it is the fund hasn't already seen it. Built from company age, press coverage in our tracked sources, whether any funding is public, and how we found it. (Being in a tracked portfolio is a hard reject, not a component — a constant is not a signal.)

Priority = 60% fit + 40% edge, both weights editable. A company reaches the shortlist only if it clears all three of: fit ≥ 70, edge ≥ 55, and coverage ≥ 50% — that last one stops a company scoring 100% on the single fact we happen to know about it.

Written by a template, not a model. It's free, instant, identical every time, cannot hallucinate, and the points add up to the headline score — so a sceptical investor can check the arithmetic. That matters, because the client will be showing these to real funds.

What runs where

One small server, two processes

Delete it, pipeline lives. │ └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Why the split. The engine is boring, deterministic Python that can be unit-tested offline in under a minute. Hermes gives a polished Telegram surface and an on-box repair loop — operational remediations are Python (founder-radar repair), adapter/layout bugs follow a playbook that is not allowed to touch scoring. If Hermes ever proves flaky, removing it costs the skill directory and one timer. The pipeline never knew it was there.

The whole run diesA heartbeat fires at +26 hours, then a repair pass at 09:05—
Fatal scan (exit 2)systemd OnFailure starts Hermes on the VPS to diagnose and patch—

Two properties make this work: every write is idempotent, so a retry can never double-write; and every stage records what it did, so a partial run resumes rather than restarts. Every row of this table is a test in the suite.
