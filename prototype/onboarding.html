Find them first.

Every morning, this hands you a small number of UK startups that are new enough to be worth an email — and tells you which fund to send each one to.

The problem it solves

Most startup lists are already old news

The obvious way to find startups is to read what venture funds have invested in, and what the tech press writes about. Both come too late. By the time a company appears on a fund's website, that fund has already given it money. You cannot introduce someone to a company they already own.

So this starts somewhere else: the UK company register. Every company in Britain has a legal birthday recorded there, the day it was created. A company born ninety days ago simply cannot be six years old. That one change is the whole idea.

Your morning

Six companies. Four minutes.

You get one company at a time, biggest opportunity first. Read it, decide, move on. There is nothing to scroll through and no list to manage — when you have seen them all, you are finished for the day.

The two numbers

Fit and Edge

Fit answers: does this company match what the fund says it invests in? Right size, right place, right stage.

Edge answers something different: how likely is it that the fund hasn't already seen this? A company nobody has written about, found straight from the register, scores high.

You need both. A perfect company that three funds already track is worth less to you than a good one nobody has found — because your job is to introduce them to something new.

When a card looks orange

It means we're guessing

To judge a company properly we want five things: what it does, where it is, how far along it is, who founded it, and whether anyone is buying. Sometimes we only know one or two.

When that happens the card gets an orange tint and says so — "we know 2 of 5 things about this company". The score might look high, but it is high on very little. Check it yourself before you email anyone.

Who you're sending to

The funds, and their hard rules

These aren't preferences — they're conditions attached to the money these funds manage. If a company breaks one, that fund legally cannot invest, no matter how good it is. The system checks these before you ever see a card, so anything that reaches you already passed.

What to expect

Honest limits

This is a research tool, not an oracle. Three things will happen regularly, and none of them means it is broken.

Some mornings will be quiet Not every day produces six good companies. An empty list is a real answer, not a bug — it is better than padding it out with weak ones.
Some cards will say the age is unknown If we can't confirm when a company started, we say so rather than guess. Those never reach the top of your list, on purpose.
Websites change and sources break Around fourteen places are checked each morning. When one changes its layout, it stops working until it's fixed — the others carry on, and the failure is recorded rather than hidden.
Why your answers matter

Every verdict makes the next list better

When you mark a company worth contacting or unsure, it is saved to Kept in this app (not only in the spreadsheet). Not for me is recorded for tuning but stays off that list.

Once there are around fifty verdicts, the system can work out where the cut-off should sit — how picky to be before something reaches you.
