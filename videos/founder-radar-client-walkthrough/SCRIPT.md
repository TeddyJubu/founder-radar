# Client walkthrough — UK Founder Radar

A screen-recording script for a non-technical client. About **6 minutes** if read unhurried (roughly 140 words a minute).

**Who speaks:** you, to the client. Warm, slow, plain. Smile without performing. Pause on the three buttons and on “unknown.”

**What to show:** the real Today page for the list, and the teaching guide’s practice cards (`/guide/`, Morning section) for the button demo — those companies are fictional, so a keep or a no does not touch the live list.

**Say out loud:** Today, Kept, Match, Fresh, Northstar, DSW, Outward, Anticus.
**Leave unsaid:** SQLite, CLI, Hermes, config hash, stage numbers, server names.

---

## 1 — The list is already there

**0:00 – 0:22** · Today, morning list in frame. Do not scroll yet.

Every morning, a short list of young UK companies is waiting for you.

Not fifty. A handful you can finish with a coffee. Sometimes none. An empty morning is a quiet day. The tool is doing its job.

---

## 2 — Why the old lists felt late

**0:22 – 0:50** · Optional still of a fund portfolio page, then back to Today.

The old way started from fund websites. Those pages show companies that already got the money. By the time a name is on a portfolio, someone else has already had the conversation.

This starts earlier. It looks for young UK companies, then asks a simpler question: which of your four funds might care, and why.

---

## 3 — What “young” means

**0:50 – 1:15** · Stay on Today. If a card shows an age, rest the cursor there.

Every UK company has a birthday on the official register. If a company is three months old, it cannot also be six years old. That birthday is how we keep the list young.

A win is small and specific. You introduce a fund to a company they have not already seen.

---

## 4 — The four funds, in one breath

**1:15 – 1:50** · A card that shows a best fund, or the guide’s funds section. One name at a time. No extra adjectives.

Four funds.

Northstar — North East.

DSW — regional UK tech, on the SEIS and EIS paths.

Outward — send it when finance is the product, or an essential part of how the product works.

Anticus — Yorkshire. The place is the rule. The sector can be broad.

The card names the fund that fits best. You still decide.

---

## 5 — Open Today and read one card

**1:50 – 3:05** · Click the first company. Pause on each item as you say it.

Open Today. This is the morning job. The spreadsheet can wait.

Each card is a company, not an article. The articles are the sources. You should not have to open them to know what you are looking at.

Read it like this.

The name, and one sentence on what they do.

Where they are, and how young they are.

Which fund fits best.

Then two scores.

**Match** — does this company fit what that fund wants? Place, stage, kind of business. Higher means a closer fit.

**Fresh** — is it still new enough that the fund might not know it yet? Younger, and less already-famous, usually means higher Fresh.

If a fact is missing, the card says **unknown**. We leave the gap. We do not fill it in.

If the card looks thin — we only know a little — treat it as thin. A pretty sentence is not the same as a fact.

---

## 6 — One, two, three

**3:05 – 3:55** · Switch to the teaching guide’s practice deck. Press the buttons for real.

Three buttons. That is the whole morning.

**One. Worth contacting.** It goes to Kept, straight away.

**Two. Unsure.** Also Kept, for later.

**Three. Not for me.** We remember that no. It will not come back on tomorrow’s Today.

Yes and maybe are your working list. No is a closed door.

The same choice lands everywhere: the website, the sheet, and your phone. If you only say “no” in chat and it is never saved, nothing changes. Press the button, or use the phone action that saves it.

---

## 7 — Kept, the phone, the sheet

**3:55 – 4:45** · Open Kept. Then a phone with the morning ping, or a still of it. Then the Google Sheet, Fund Criteria tab, two seconds, and leave.

Kept is the shelf. Open it when you want the companies you already said yes or maybe to. The nos are stored, and they are not on this shelf.

Your phone gets a short ping when the list is ready. Tap through to Today. A keep or a no from the phone counts when it is saved — same shelf, same list.

The Google Sheet is where the rules live: which funds, what matters more, age limits, which sources are on. After each morning it also gets a copy of the results. For the ten-minute pass, stay on Today.

---

## 8 — What happened before you woke up

**4:45 – 5:35** · Back to Today. No architecture diagram unless you want a single still of “one notebook, three windows.”

At half past six, London time, a computer wakes up.

It reads the rules from the sheet.

It gathers clues. Some clues are companies a person already picked — a university spinout, an accelerator, a grant, a UK tech story. Some clues are just a recent birthday on the register. The register proves age. It does not prove the company is good.

Long articles get turned into short facts.

The same company, found twice, becomes one card.

Then the scores. Hard rules first, then the numbers. Same facts, same scores, every time. That step does not use a chat model, and it does not browse the web.

One last check can take a clearly wrong card off the list — already funded, too late, or not UK. It cannot add a company, and it cannot change a score.

Then you get the ping.

---

## 9 — If something disappeared

**5:35 – 6:05** · Hold on a card, or on an empty Today.

If a company drops off, the reason is a rule or a number you can check. Ask, and we can show the sum. It is not “the computer felt differently this morning.”

Some mornings the list is empty. That is allowed. The tool should not invent companies to fill the page.

---

## 10 — Close

**6:05 – 6:25** · Today, full list. End card if you want one: *One list. Three buttons. Scores you can check.*

So. Open Today. Read the card. Press one, two, or three.

One notebook behind it all. The sheet, the site, and your phone are windows onto that same list.

A list that is actually young — and a score you can ask to see.

Cut sections 2 and 8. What remains is the job: open Today, read Match and Fresh, press one two or three, trust an empty morning. About three and a half minutes.
